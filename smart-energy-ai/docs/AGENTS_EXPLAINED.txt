AGENT 1: ENERGY MONITORING & ANALYSIS AGENT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. What is the agent?
   The historical data analytics specialist responsible for evaluating past energy usage.
2. Why does it exist?
   Before you can optimize or forecast energy, you must accurately understand current
   and historical consumption patterns.
3. What does it receive?
   A validated Pandas DataFrame with chronological timestamps and hourly kWh readings.
4. What does it do?
   Computes total energy, daily average, hourly diurnal curves, peak hour, base trough,
   weekday vs weekend variances, and overall trend slopes.
5. What tools does it use?
   `services/energy_calculator.py` (NumPy & Pandas aggregations) + `services/llm_service.py`.
6. What does it output?
   A structured dictionary containing exact metrics and an executive natural language summary.
7. How does it communicate?
   Returns its dictionary to Agent 6 (Coordinator), which shares the baseline metrics with
   Cost and Appliance agents.
8. Real-life analogy:
   Like an accountant who audits your bank statements to tell you where your money went.
9. Technical explanation:
   Performs descriptive statistical profiling, linear polynomial regression for slope/trend,
   and 24-hour diurnal grouping.
10. One-line viva answer:
    "Agent 1 deterministically calculates historical consumption metrics, peak demand windows,
     and diurnal usage profiles."

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
AGENT 2: FORECASTING AGENT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. What is the agent?
   The predictive machine learning specialist that models upcoming electricity demand.
2. Why does it exist?
   To alert consumers and utility planners ahead of time when peak demand will occur.
3. What does it receive?
   Historical time-series sequence and a forecast horizon (default: 24 hours).
4. What does it do?
   Engineers autoregressive features (lags and rolling averages), fits a Random Forest Regressor,
   measures validation error (MAE, RMSE, MAPE), and predicts future 24h demand.
5. What tools does it use?
   `services/forecasting.py` (`scikit-learn` Random Forest) + `services/llm_service.py`.
6. What does it output?
   Predicted hourly sequence, peak forecast time, model accuracy scores, and LLM advice.
7. How does it communicate?
   Provides anticipated peak hours to Agent 6 so load-shifting advice can be aligned with
   expected grid stress.
8. Real-life analogy:
   Like a meteorologist who forecasts tomorrow's weather so you know if you need an umbrella.
9. Technical explanation:
   Uses supervised ensemble learning on lagged autoregressive vectors (t-1, t-2, t-24)
   with chronological holdout validation.
10. One-line viva answer:
    "Agent 2 employs a Random Forest Regressor on autoregressive lag features to project 24-hour
     demand and validate accuracy via MAE and RMSE."

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
AGENT 3: APPLIANCE OPTIMIZATION AGENT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. What is the agent?
   The appliance disaggregation and load-shifting scheduler.
2. Why does it exist?
   Consumers need actionable advice on specific devices (e.g. when to run washing machines or ACs).
3. What does it receive?
   Appliance catalog (wattage, usage hours, flexibility) and active electricity tariff tiers.
4. What does it do?
   Identifies high-power loads, separates flexible from inflexible devices, and schedules
   heavy flexible loads into off-peak hours.
5. What tools does it use?
   `services/optimization.py` + `services/llm_service.py`.
6. What does it output?
   Device-level breakdown, recommended operating schedules, and potential kWh saved.
7. How does it communicate?
   Feeds appliance cost and savings potential into Agent 4 (Cost Agent) and Agent 6 (Coordinator).
8. Real-life analogy:
   Like an efficiency coach who schedules your workouts and chores at the best times of day.
9. Technical explanation:
   Applies heuristic load-shifting algorithms, mapping deferrable energy requirements
   to off-peak tariff periods.
10. One-line viva answer:
    "Agent 3 classifies appliances by flexibility and reschedules deferrable loads away from peak
     tariff windows to reduce peak coincident demand."

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
AGENT 4: COST OPTIMIZATION AGENT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. What is the agent?
   The financial and tariff calculation specialist.
2. Why does it exist?
   Consumers ultimately care about their monetary electricity bill and ROI.
3. What does it receive?
   Consumption data, appliance load profiles, and `TariffConfig` (base, peak, off-peak rates).
4. What does it do?
   Computes Time-of-Day (ToD) electricity costs, compares Current vs Optimized bills,
   and calculates net savings in INR (₹) and percentage improvement.
5. What tools does it use?
   `services/optimization.py` + `services/llm_service.py`.
6. What does it output?
   Detailed cost metrics, projected savings in Rupees, and percentage bill reduction.
7. How does it communicate?
   Supplies financial quantification to Agent 6 for inclusion in the final report.
8. Real-life analogy:
   Like a tax consultant who finds legal deductions to lower your tax bill.
9. Technical explanation:
   Computes piece-wise Time-of-Day tariff integrals and prorated fixed charges to evaluate
   cost deltas between unmanaged and load-shifted regimes.
10. One-line viva answer:
    "Agent 4 models Time-of-Day and tiered tariffs to quantify projected monthly financial savings
     from load optimization."

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
AGENT 5: ANOMALY & ENERGY SAFETY AGENT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. What is the agent?
   The behavioral watchdog and energy waste auditing specialist.
2. Why does it exist?
   To detect unexpected consumption leaks, continuous equipment operation, or data anomalies.
3. What does it receive?
   Historical time-series with hour-of-day features.
4. What does it do?
   Calculates hour-specific diurnal Z-scores, fits an Isolation Forest, identifies outliers,
   assigns severity, and generates safe troubleshooting guidance.
5. What tools does it use?
   `services/anomaly_detection.py` (Isolation Forest & Z-Score) + `services/llm_service.py`.
6. What does it output?
   List of flagged anomalies (timestamp, measured value, normal range, explanation, severity).
7. How does it communicate?
   Warns Agent 6 so unusual spikes are considered when formulating the overall energy plan.
8. Real-life analogy:
   Like a home smoke detector that beeps when something unusual is happening in the kitchen.
9. Technical explanation:
   Combines parametric diurnal Z-score thresholding with non-parametric Isolation Forest
   ensemble trees to isolate multivariate behavioral outliers.
10. One-line viva answer:
    "Agent 5 detects unusual consumption surges using a hybrid diurnal Z-score and Isolation Forest
     approach, providing non-invasive safety advisories."

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
AGENT 6: ENERGY ADVISOR / COORDINATOR AGENT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. What is the agent?
   The central orchestrator and strategic synthesizer of the entire multi-agent system.
2. Why does it exist?
   Without a coordinator, the 5 specialist agents would produce isolated numbers that
   confuse the user. The coordinator harmonizes the entire pipeline into one coherent strategy.
3. What does it receive?
   User queries, dataset references, and the structured outputs from all 5 specialized agents.
4. What does it do?
   Routes user intents, executes the pipeline, resolves conflicting recommendations,
   and formats the comprehensive Final Energy Management Report.
5. What tools does it use?
   Agent orchestrator pipeline + `services/llm_service.py` synthesis prompts.
6. What does it output?
   Unified system state, chatbot conversational responses, and downloadable audit reports.
7. How does it communicate?
   Acts as the central communication hub; calls all other agents and collates their responses.
8. Real-life analogy:
   Like the managing director of a hospital who consults cardiologists, radiologists, and
   surgeons before giving the patient a final diagnosis.
9. Technical explanation:
   Implements hierarchical multi-agent orchestration with dynamic query routing, structured
   state merging, and grounded prompt synthesis.
10. One-line viva answer:
    "Agent 6 orchestrates the multi-agent pipeline, routes user inquiries, resolves conflicting
     recommendations, and synthesizes the unified strategic audit report."

EXPLAIN ALL 6 AGENTS IN 2 MINUTES (DEEP SUMMARY FOR VIVA)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
"In SmartEnergy AI, we avoided the monolithic approach of using a single LLM to do
everything. Instead, we designed a hierarchical 6-agent architecture where deterministic
computation, machine learning, and natural language reasoning are cleanly decoupled.

Agent 1, the Monitoring Agent, processes smart-meter time series to deterministically
extract statistical baselines, 24-hour diurnal curves, and weekday versus weekend variances.

Agent 2, the Forecasting Agent, applies supervised Random Forest regression using autoregressive
lag features—such as t-1, t-2, and t-24—to project upcoming 24-hour demand and validates
its own accuracy using Mean Absolute Error and RMSE.

Agent 3, the Appliance Optimization Agent, disaggregates domestic loads into flexible and
critical categories, constructing optimized operating schedules that avoid high-tariff periods.

Agent 4, the Cost Optimization Agent, applies Time-of-Day and slab electricity tariffs to
mathematically demonstrate the bill reduction between unmanaged and load-shifted regimes.

Agent 5, the Anomaly & Safety Agent, utilizes a hybrid approach combining hour-specific
Z-score bounds with scikit-learn's Isolation Forest to flag unusual late-night surges and
consumption anomalies, offering safe, non-invasive advice.

Finally, Agent 6, the Coordinator Agent, directs this workflow, manages conversational intent
routing in our assistant chat tab, and compiles the final comprehensive audit report."
